Published research

On Convex Kinematics:
A Resurrection of Holditch’s Theorem

With Sawyer Jacobson
Parabola, Vol. 61 No. 2 · August 2025 · UNSW

Holditch's Theorem is a strange, old fact about convex shapes: slide a fixed-length chord all the way around the boundary of any convex region, and a point riding along that chord traces out a smaller region. However oddly the outer shape is drawn, the two areas always relate by one clean formula. Reverend Holditch's original 1857 proof runs about ten lines but explains almost nothing about why it's true. Sawyer and I rebuilt the result on more concrete ground instead — using Green's Theorem to handle area along a curve, and an unexpected triangle, tied together by Stewart's Theorem, to make the whole construction visible step by step.

Explore the proof (PDF)

Written over Thanksgiving break in 2024. Reviewed by Dr. Bill Dunham at Bryn Mawr College before publication submission.

04 / The person behind the projects

WILLIAM BALZ BOSTON, MA

Curiosity, in practice.

Work first.
Credentials later.

I'm a sophomore studying Electrical Engineering and Biomedical Engineering at Tufts University, after a high school career built around moving fast: I taught myself a full year of Calculus BC in under a month and walked into the AP exam cold, scoring a 5, later taking Linear Algebra and Multivariable Calculus at Harvard. I also took junior-level EE Probability & Statistics at Tufts as the only freshman in the room.

That same instinct — work first, credentials later — carried into everywhere I've worked since: optimizing manufacturing processes in metal 3D-printing at VulcanForms, founding an ed-tech startup from inside a venture fund at Morningside, and mentoring new researchers at Tufts' cellular agriculture lab. It started, though, with a proof.

“I remember sitting at my desk on the phone with my friend during our Thanksgiving break. It was the 40th hour that week we'd spent trying to prove Holditch's Theorem in a more intuitive, concretely geometric format. We were sold on using Green's Theorem, but hadn't made much progress until a last-ditch effort late on Sunday night: an implicit triangle that would tie everything together with Stewart's Theorem.”

— My sappy recounting of the creative process

The same pattern showed up again at VulcanForms, when a mentor left for three days and asked for an optimized laser-marking protocol by the time he got back — thousands of settings, no obvious way to test them all. And again at Morningside, when a stalled prototype turned into two days of learning Python and building the thing myself. Different fields, same move: narrow an enormous space of options down to the one that actually works, fast.
